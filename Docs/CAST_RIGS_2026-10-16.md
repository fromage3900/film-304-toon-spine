next step. Everything here went through a builder; nothing was hand-edited in
the editor, per `Docs/CONTENT_CONVENTIONS.md`.

decisions instead of the code:

## The three findings that mattered

**1. Both human rigs hide their own body.** `David_Body_GEO` and `Sam_Body_GEO`
are fully skinned (skinCluster105 / 152, 18,432 tris each) but the artist left
`visibility = false` in the saved scene. The exporter's visibility discipline —
inherited from the proven Dana exporter — correctly refused them, and the report
named the blocker instead of silently exporting a headless character. The fix is
an explicit, named `include_hidden_regex` in the spec, recorded in the report as
`visibility_overridden`. It stays narrow: the unbound siblings
(`*_GEOBase`, `*_GEOBase1`, `*_PartialBody_*`) are still excluded because they
carry no skinCluster.

**2. Selecting an invisible node is not the same as showing it.** The first
consolidated FBX came out with the body missing entirely. Maya's FBX carries the
node's Visibility flag, and Blender's importer honours it, so the body was
dropped downstream. Worse, on these rigs the flag is *driven by a rig
expression* — "hide the body while working on clothes" — so the attribute arrives
locked and `setAttr` raises. The exporter now breaks the driving connection on
the node and its ancestors, in memory. The scene is never saved.

**3. The legacy UE FBX importer creates one SkeletalMesh per armature and drops
the rest.** `mesh.import_mesh` reported `total_imported: 1` for David and
produced a single mesh named `David_Shirt_GEO`. An independent read of the same
file with Blender showed why: David and Sam carry **two** armatures
(`DeformationSystem` 101 bones, `FaceDeformationSystem` 64 bones), unlike Dana's
single 1,230-bone skeleton.

`Python/import_cast_rigs.py` exists to make that visible — it reports every asset
the importer actually creates instead of accepting a single-item result.

(This one turned out to be a second, smaller problem stacked on the first: the
armature split limits how many SkeletalMeshes can be produced, but the missing
skin is an upstream exporter problem. Both are recorded separately below.)

**4. A bound slot can still be wrong.** The first Sam bind reported
`null slots: []` and looked clean, while three eye slots were quietly carrying
`WorldGridMaterial` — the engine default the FBX importer substitutes. The
verifier now reports `engine_default_slots` separately, because "not null" is not
"correct". Sam's spec also had no eye roles at all, so those slots never had a
pattern to match; the spec now covers eyes, cornea, teeth and mouth.

## Break room — staged and verified

`L_Toon_Shot_BreakRoom`: 16 meshes, 3 cameras (`Cam_BR_SH090`, `Cam_BR_SH100`,
`Cam_BR_SH110`), 3 practicals plus sun/sky. Verifier: **0 problems**, every
placed mesh's world AABB inside the room.

The room is 111.2 × 54.6 × 60 on the ZMJ kit's own authored scale. `BR_Shell` is
placed at (64.3, 46.3, 8.2) because `SM_BreakRoomTogether_ZMJ` has local bounds
origin (-64.3, -46.3, 21.8) with extent (55.6, 27.3, 30); that is the exact
re-centre, not a rounded guess.

The verifier caught a bug in itself first: it ignored the asset's own bounds
origin and reported every placement as ~64 units out of the room. Fixed before
the run was called good.

### Not placed, on purpose

- `SM_O_CounterTop_ZMJ` — 3.2 × 0.2 × 5.8 against a 58.8-unit counter. Needs a
  ~20× scale decision; guessing it is an art call.
- Six cabinet doors and six knobs — identical flat panels at the origin, no frame
  in the kit to align them to.
- `SM_O_BreakRoomFloor/Wall_ZMJ` — superseded by `BR_Shell`, which already
  carries floor and walls at the same footprint.
- The KS / SG / Osc / KMP props — different authored scale (`SM_P_FilingCabinets_SG`
  is 322 × 174 × 275 against this room's 111 × 55). They need per-mesh scale
  decisions, and most belong to the office set anyway.

## Blockers, named

1. **David and Sam are missing their skin, hair, teeth and tongue.** Both arrive
   as 6 material slots out of 11 and 12.

   The loss happens in **Maya's FBX exporter**, not in Blender and not in UE, and
   that was worth establishing rather than assuming. Three independent reads
   agree:

   | Read | Result |
   |---|---|
   | Maya export census (`export_david.json`) | 16 meshes selected, 44,722 tris |
   | Blender FBX import of that file | 10 meshes, 6 materials |
   | Raw byte scan of the FBX | `David_Body_GEO` occurs **0** times; `David_Shirt_GEO` occurs once |

   So the body is never written into the file. Everything Blender and UE do is
   downstream of a file that is already missing it. This is not fixed by any
   setting in this repo's toolchain.

   The dropped meshes (body, hair, upper/lower teeth, tongue) are the ones bound
   across the rig's two armature systems; the surviving ten sit cleanly on one
   system or the other. Opening `David_Rig_v1.51.mb` in the Maya GUI and using
   **File > Export Selection** on `David_Body_GEO` alone will confirm or refute
   this in a couple of minutes, which is the fastest next step available.

   **Concretely: David and Sam have no skin and no hair in Unreal.** They must
   not be shot in a close-up until this is resolved, and the fix is owned by the
   rig artist, not by this pipeline.
2. **No visual judgement.** The editor viewport reports 0×0 on this machine, so
   the break room is numerically verified but not seen. Per
   `Docs/HANDOFF_2026-10-08_NEXT_PHASE.md` work order 2, stills need the SM6
   workstation.
3. **Maya morph targets.** `Blink_L` / `Blink_R` exist in Dana's FBX but not in
   the asset — the legacy importer ran with `bImportMorphTargets` off. Blink is
   carried by the lid bones, so it still moves lashes, but this is inherited, not
   fixed here.
